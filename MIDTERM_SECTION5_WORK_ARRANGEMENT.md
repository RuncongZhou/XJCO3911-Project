# 五、下一部分的工作安排

The remaining research work is organized around four interconnected objectives: (1) systematic ablation analysis and scalability evaluation; (2) algorithm enhancement through joint optimization and hybrid strategies; (3) platform refinement and extended visualization capabilities; and (4) dissertation writing and defense preparation. These objectives are designed to address the current limitations of the study and to produce a complete, high-quality research output within the stipulated timeline.

The first priority in the next stage is to conduct a thorough and systematic ablation analysis of the partition algorithms. The current experiments have compared HiveMind and EdgePipe under varying models, device count, bandwidth, and device performance, but the contribution of each factor to the throughput–latency trade-off has not been isolated. Controlled ablation experiments will be designed to vary one factor at a time while holding others fixed, enabling the quantification of the marginal impact of each configuration parameter. Furthermore, scalability tests will be conducted on larger and deeper DNN models (e.g., ResNet-50, ResNet-101) to assess whether the observed trends generalize beyond the current set of models. This analysis is expected to yield a rigorous understanding of when and why each algorithm outperforms the other, which will directly inform the design of algorithmic improvements and the formulation of practical design guidelines for edge deployment.

The second objective concerns the exploration of algorithmic enhancements to address the identified limitations. Based on the current findings, two primary directions will be pursued. First, the device ordering is currently fixed or randomly assigned; however, the assignment of devices to pipeline stages can significantly affect both throughput and latency when devices are heterogeneous. A joint optimization framework will be investigated, in which partition and device ordering are optimized together rather than sequentially. This approach may yield partitions that better exploit device heterogeneity. Second, a hybrid strategy will be explored that combines HiveMind and EdgePipe: for example, using HiveMind to obtain an initial partition and then refining it with EdgePipe-style stage balancing, or developing a Pareto-optimal selection mechanism that switches between algorithms based on runtime workload characteristics (throughput-bound vs. latency-bound). The proposed enhancements will be evaluated through controlled experiments and compared against the baseline algorithms.

Concurrently, the web-based visualization platform will be further refined to improve its utility for research and demonstration. Specific planned improvements include the implementation of a batch export module that allows users to export experiment configurations and results to CSV or JSON for offline analysis and reproducibility; the addition of a communication-overhead visualization that explicitly displays per-stage transfer times and identifies communication bottlenecks in the partition scheme; and the extension of the algorithm comparison view to support side-by-side visualization of partition boundaries across multiple configurations. These enhancements will strengthen the platform's role as both a research tool and a demonstration vehicle for the thesis. Finally, the formal dissertation writing process will commence in parallel with the above experimental work, following the prescribed thesis structure. The dissertation will systematically document the research motivation, theoretical background of pipeline partitioning and edge inference, experimental methodology, quantitative results, and critical analysis, incorporating all findings from both the mid-term and final-stage experiments. The defense preparation will include the completion of presentation slides, rehearsal of the defense script, and preparation of anticipated questions and answers.
