# 四、毕业论文中存在的问题

The current thesis work has several limitations that need to be acknowledged and addressed in the remaining stages of the project. These issues span the evaluation methodology, the algorithmic scope, the platform capabilities, and the experimental coverage.

First, the evaluation is based entirely on simulation rather than real deployment. The platform uses pre-profiled layer Flops and DataSize to compute computation time and transfer time analytically, without running actual DNN inference on physical devices. As a result, the reported throughput and inference time may differ from real-world performance due to factors such as network jitter, hardware variance, memory bandwidth constraints, and runtime scheduling overhead. The simulation model assumes ideal pipeline execution and does not account for batch scheduling, buffer management, or synchronization delays that arise in practical distributed systems. Validation on real edge devices or at least a more detailed simulation that incorporates these factors would strengthen the credibility of the experimental conclusions.

Second, the system assumes a linear pipeline topology in which devices are arranged in a fixed sequential chain. More complex topologies, such as tree-structured pipelines, graph-based device networks, or multi-branch parallel inference, are not yet supported. Many real-world edge deployments involve heterogeneous device clusters with non-linear connectivity, and the current framework cannot model or optimize for such scenarios. Extending the partition algorithms and the platform to support arbitrary topologies would broaden the applicability of the research.

Third, the device ordering—the assignment of physical devices to pipeline stages—is currently fixed or randomly assigned. When devices are heterogeneous in terms of compute capability and network connectivity, the ordering can significantly affect both throughput and latency. The partition algorithms optimize layer assignment given a fixed ordering, but they do not jointly optimize partition and device ordering. This limitation may lead to suboptimal solutions when device heterogeneity is pronounced. Joint optimization of partition and device ordering is planned as future work but has not yet been implemented.

Fourth, the experimental evaluation lacks systematic ablation analysis and scalability tests. While the current experiments vary models, device count, bandwidth, and device performance, the marginal contribution of each factor to the throughput–latency trade-off has not been isolated through controlled ablation. Furthermore, the models evaluated (AlexNet, Vgg19, YOLONet, SqueezeNet) are relatively small; it remains unclear whether the observed trends generalize to larger and deeper architectures such as ResNet-50 or ResNet-101. Ablation studies and scalability experiments on a broader set of models would provide a more rigorous and generalizable characterization of the algorithms.

Finally, the platform has room for improvement in terms of batch export, reproducibility, and visualization. There is no built-in mechanism to export experiment configurations and results in bulk for offline analysis or to reproduce a large set of experiments from a configuration file. The communication-overhead visualization, which would help users identify bottlenecks in the partition scheme, has not yet been implemented. These enhancements would improve the platform's utility as a research tool and strengthen the thesis demonstration.
